## Authorization, goals and evidence

An ethical assessment begins with explicit owner permission, the systems and identities in scope, permitted techniques, time window, stop conditions and an agreed reporting audience. White/black/gray labels describe a source's classification; the authorization document determines what an operator may do. Black-box and white-box describe supplied knowledge rather than employee status or tool choice. A novice using an existing script is not automatically malicious.

Confidentiality protects against unauthorized disclosure, integrity against unauthorized change, and availability supports timely authorized use. A signature supplies key-based integrity/origin evidence; the attribution and nonrepudiation claim also needs trustworthy identity binding and key custody. SLE = asset value × exposure factor; the supplied $100,000 × 0.5 example gives $50,000. Annual loss expectancy additionally needs a defensible annual frequency.

## Models and response

The five-phase hacking mnemonic, the Cyber Kill Chain and the Diamond Model answer different questions. In the kill chain, reconnaissance precedes weaponization, delivery, exploitation, installation, C2 and actions on objectives. In the Diamond Model, adversary, capability, infrastructure and victim describe an intrusion event. Reporting and authorized cleanup remain necessary even when a five-phase mnemonic omits them.

Preparation is the first phase in the older four-phase incident-response model. NIST SP 800-61r3 now connects incident response to CSF 2.0 risk management; name the model/version before answering a phase question. Triage correlates alerts with evidence. Containment limits ongoing harm; eradication removes the cause; recovery restores trusted operation. A BCP protects critical business functions during disruption.

## Trust and governance

ISO/IEC 27001:2022 is the supplied ISMS-requirements reference; 27002 supplies control guidance and 27005 risk-management guidance. The supplied zero-trust image emphasizes identity verification, device verification and least privilege. These are useful dimensions; an access decision must also consider the requested resource and current policy/context. NIST's principle is that location or ownership alone grants no implicit trust. HIPAA's applicability depends on covered-entity/business-associate status, not merely a healthcare setting.

Example: an alert correctly identifying an authorized exploit is still a true positive for detection evaluation. The exercise's authorization and the sensor's classification are separate evidence dimensions. AI-supported scale or forecasting likewise needs coverage and error measurements before it establishes operational benefit.

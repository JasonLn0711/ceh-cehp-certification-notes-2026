## Identification, classification and applicability

Keep the chain asset → observed component/configuration → candidate weakness → applicability → permitted impact evidence → priority → repair → verification. A scanner alert is a candidate finding. A version string may be stale, forged or affected by backported fixes. Confirm the exact advisory, build, reachable feature, privileges and compensating controls. A public exploit provides context; it is neither proof that this asset is exploitable nor permission to run it.

CVE identifies a particular published vulnerability record; CWE classifies a weakness type; CPE names a product/platform representation used in applicability matching. CVSS summarizes technical severity under a stated version/vector. Add exposure, business criticality, current threat evidence and repair constraints for local priority. A high score alone does not prove exploitation, active compromise or a mandatory forensic audit.

## Assessment dimensions

Internal/external describe vantage. Network/host/application describe coverage. Credentialed/unauthenticated describe access context. Automated/manual describe execution method. These dimensions can coexist: an internal network-based scan may be credentialed. Successful scanner logins and local registry/patch detail are stronger evidence of credentialed checks than duration alone. A focused HTTP scanner and a broad vulnerability platform have overlapping functions; edition/version claims require vendor evidence.

## Lifecycle and reporting

Discovery/inventory identifies scope. Analysis validates findings. Risk assessment prioritizes them. Remediation changes the system. Verification repeats the relevant checks after repair. The source's long narratives sometimes span all these phases; answer according to the activity actually asked. Report scope/method/tools in the assessment overview, reproducible observations in findings, priority/impact reasoning in risk assessment and proposed repairs in recommendations.

Virtual patching is a tested compensating control while a permanent fix is pending. A WAF only covers attack paths it can inspect and block. Active compromise additionally requires incident response. Fuzzing explores input behavior with structured or unstructured generation/mutation; it is broader than massive random input. Non-destructive controls are usually sufficient to establish injection or a configuration defect; deleting a real database is unnecessary proof.

Example: a scanner reports SSH RCE at CVSS 9.0. Check the exact build/advisory, exposure and feature configuration; then choose a safe proof or repair with the owner. After repair, repeat the relevant test and retain the earlier evidence.

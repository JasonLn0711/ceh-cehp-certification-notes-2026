## Collection method and confidence

Passive collection uses already available observations; active collection sends requests/probes or communicates with a person. Search engines, registration data, public social posts, existing mail and archived pages can provide clues. A DNS query to a public resolver and a query sent directly to the target have different interaction paths. A third-party tool is not automatically passive: inspect how it collects. Port scanning is active even when the address is public.

Separate observation from inference. A job posting naming a database product suggests a possible deployment; it does not prove a particular production version. Matching usernames across sites are leads rather than authenticated identity. Geotags may expose routines but may also be stale, fabricated or unrelated. Sender headers supplied by an untrusted party are weaker than Received fields added by a trusted mail system.

## Search operators and archives

site: restricts indexed sites, inurl: matches URL text, intitle: matches titles and filetype: restricts a document type. A matching admin URL does not prove that authentication is absent. S3 references in HTML and indexed pages suggest bucket names/URLs; ownership and public read/write permissions remain separate tests. Alerts monitor newly indexed matching content rather than the complete web.

The Deep Web means material absent from ordinary search indexes; Tor hidden services are a different access/naming context. A site:onion expression does not give a normal search engine comprehensive access to hidden services. Google's cache: operator is retired. Use an available archive with an explicit snapshot date, then distinguish historical content from today's exposure.

## Tools and study use

The source associates Sherlock with username lookup, Creepy with geotag mapping, FOCA with document metadata and Shodan with internet-service observations. Those are role associations, with present support/version limits left open where unverified. A proxy/intermediary changes the address visible at the target but does not guarantee anonymity.

Example: a historical PDF contains a username and software string. Record document date, retrieval source and the exact metadata field; verify whether the account/software still exists before assigning risk. Do not turn public information into a claim of current compromise.

## Sensor vantage and detection logic

HIDS sees local file/process/configuration events. NIDS observes traffic at its network position. IDS primarily reports; an inline IPS may block. Signature recognition matches known patterns; anomaly detection compares expected behavior; stateful protocol analysis checks valid protocol state. A true positive is an alert on ground-truth attack activity, a false positive an alert on ground-truth benign activity, a false negative a missed attack and a true negative correctly unflagged benign activity. An authorized red-team payload can be valid ground truth for testing the sensor.

A packet filter uses packet attributes. A stateful firewall tracks flows. Application inspection understands relevant protocol content. Product labels such as multilayer can overlap; checking session state alone does not prove payload inspection. Encrypted traffic may conceal payload while leaving endpoint and flow metadata visible.

## Reassembly and evasion

Insertion presents bytes accepted by the sensor but discarded by the endpoint. Evasion presents meaningful bytes accepted by the endpoint but missed by the sensor. IP fragmentation and TCP segmentation/session splicing are different layers; each requires correct reconstruction. Normalize/check checksums and reassemble consistently before applying signatures. Anomaly detection can supplement this process rather than replace it. Tiny fragments attempt to split meaningful headers across pieces.

Tunneling carries another protocol through an allowed one. HTTP, DNS, ICMP and ACK/covert channels need actual protocol evidence to identify them. Low-rate encoded traffic alone is not uniquely DNS. Firewalking uses TTL-dependent observations to infer forwarding rules and remains subject to filtering/loss. MAC spoofing, decoys and a proxy affect attribution in different ways.

## Decoys and response

Low-interaction decoys emulate limited services; high-interaction environments allow deeper actions on real or realistic systems. Terms such as pure honeypot are taxonomy-dependent. Uniform response times or protocol mismatches suggest emulation but do not prove it. A research honeynet must remain contained.

Example: an IDS flags the original exploit signature but misses a behavior-equivalent rewrite. That shows a tested signature limitation, not failure of every detection layer. Retain endpoint events, protocol reconstruction and ground-truth timing to determine what actually happened.

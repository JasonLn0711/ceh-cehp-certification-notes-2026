## Separate discovery, authentication and encryption

Listening for beacons is passive radio discovery; sending probes is active. airodump-ng captures traffic, aireplay-ng injects supported frames, aircrack-ng performs compatible key analysis and airmon-ng manages monitor-mode setup. A GUI can combine roles; its name does not prove current adapter/OS support. A rogue AP is unapproved; an evil twin additionally impersonates a trusted network. A fake SSID alone does not defeat TLS or properly validated enterprise authentication.

WEP uses RC4 with a short 24-bit IV and has fundamental reuse/integrity problems. WPA/TKIP is a different legacy construction. WPA2 commonly uses AES-CCMP, but WPA3 can also exhibit AES/CCMP and a four-way exchange: inspect the advertised authentication/key-management suite. WPA2-PSK handshakes permit offline candidate checking, not recovery of a strong random secret by observation alone. Deauthentication may induce reconnection when protections permit it; a capture is not itself a weak-key finding.

KRACK exploits key reinstallation causing nonce/replay-state reuse on vulnerable implementations. It does not reveal the password. WPA3-Personal's SAE changes the password-authenticated exchange and resists passive offline guessing under its protocol assumptions. A WPA2 downgrade needs transition/fallback conditions; pure WPA3 cannot be universally forced to WPA2. Centralized per-identity access requires an enterprise authentication design such as 802.1X/EAP with appropriate server validation, not merely a WPA3 label.

## Backend and radio boundaries

Wireless EAP/EAPOL and backend RADIUS are separate layers. A captured over-the-air exchange does not automatically expose RADIUS or create a successful replay. A local bridge may inherit weakly bound network admission, so validate session/device binding. RF jamming disrupts communication and differs from replaying an accepted signal. Device range/battery specifications do not uniquely identify an unknown jammer model.

Bluejacking sends unsolicited Bluetooth messages; bluesnarfing retrieves data; rooting is a different device privilege change. Nondiscoverable mode reduces casual discovery but is not universal protection. BLE link-encryption handshake parameters do not replace a missing LTK; a hijacking tool cannot generically decrypt any encrypted capture.

Example: an AP advertises the same name as the hospital network. Confirm authorization, wired attachment, security suite and server-certificate validation. Those observations distinguish a rogue/equivalent name from a successful evil-twin credential interception.

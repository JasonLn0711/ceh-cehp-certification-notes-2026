## Q01 — D

The client-task result directly describes whether the selected users could complete the service action in the fixture. Source-address counts describe traffic distribution, and a processor reading describes one resource. Neither alone establishes user impact. A larger reply describes an amplification property, which may be relevant to an incident but does not measure a user's task. Keep the observation window, task definition, and selected population with the success result. Five successes out of one hundred stipulated checks supports that sample; it does not establish the experience of every user. These records are simulated output. Availability impact also does not determine malicious intent, identify a person, or prove that a particular resource caused the failure. Those are separate conclusions requiring their own evidence.

## Q02 — B

## Q04 — C

Sixty divided by six thousand is zero point zero one, or one percent. The stipulated unique counters and common window make that calculation meaningful here. Sixty is a count, so it cannot become sixty percent without a denominator. Backlog capacity measures available state, not the number of attempts. A low completion fraction does not prove that the remaining source addresses were forged or that all failures were malicious. Connection progress, state occupancy, and user success help identify a resource problem; attribution requires other evidence. In live telemetry, check counter definitions and interval boundaries before applying the same division. Attempts that cross an interval boundary or repeated records can otherwise change its meaning. Our result describes the constructed window rather than an observed live server.

## Q05 — D

The first two alternatives mix an Ethernet count with an Internet Protocol count, so they do not answer a consistent-layer question. The third uses Ethernet for both sides and can describe that separate byte convention, but it is not the requested layer and does not equal fifty. State the numerator, denominator, layer, and pairing rule when reporting amplification. The fixture constructs the reflector-side request and response. A victim-ingress record would be a separate observation point. Their sizes demonstrate the fixture's byte relationship, not a measured traffic rate, real attack volume, or control effectiveness. ## Q07 — C

## Q08 — B

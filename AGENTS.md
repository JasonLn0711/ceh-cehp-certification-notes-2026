# AGENTS.md

This repo is the canonical learning workspace for Jason's 2026 CEH13 + CEHP
course and exam preparation.

## Mission

Build a focused CEH / CEHP preparation record through one integrated,
historical-incident side project per active learning week. Focused research,
class notes, and assessments remove blockers and verify the weekly result.

## FIRST PRINCIPLE

- Scarce resource: Jason's certification study capacity before and during the
  October CEH and November CEHP classes.
- Canonical home: this repo owns the weekly incident projects, technical study
  notes, assessments, weak-topic repair, practice logs, and readiness evidence.
- Planning role: `planning-everything-track` owns calendar capacity,
  registration status, confirmed discount, payment / opening notice gates, and
  thin locator/status mirrors only.
- Scope control: keep this repo educational, lawful, authorized, and
  certification-focused. Private registration identifiers, payment data,
  credentials, target-system details, and activity outside the authorized
  training scope stay in their protected homes outside this learning workspace.

## Writing Voice

- Write durable notes, source analyses, planning bridges, and study docs in a
  confident, generous, positive-scope voice.
- Start from purpose, capability, evidence, ownership, scope control, and next
  action.
- Convert 否定語氣、防守語氣、防衛語氣 into affirmative capability,
  boundary, activation, and stewardship language before publication.
- Preserve exact source wording in transcripts, emails, quoted material, legal
  status, safety language, and credential controls when precision requires it.

## Working Rules

1. Keep notes plain Markdown.
2. Use official course scope as the backbone before adding outside resources.
3. Start each active week with one bounded incident-project action and acceptance
   check. Research the first observed blocker, apply the result, and rerun.
4. Use explanations and posttests after execution evidence as diagnostic
   support. Every posttest produces `pass`, `review`, or
   `activation_needed`.
5. Keep lab notes local, lawful, and training-focused. Use toy examples,
   authorized labs, or official training environments only.
6. Keep the knowledge surface focused on CEH + CEHP course readiness; broader
   cybersecurity topics enter only when they support the official course scope.
7. For complete learning packets, follow
   `docs/day-01-03-learning-package-rule.md`: include study guide, worksheet,
   pretest questions, separate pretest answer key, posttest questions, separate
   posttest answer key, CEH exam-focus advice, traceability map, and closeout.
8. Keep `projects/weekly-incident-projects/README.md` as the project index.
   The ISO week is the minimum project unit, and active learning lanes share
   one integrated side project for that week.
9. Record project state truthfully as `planned`, `attempted`, `blocked`, or
   `accepted`. A prompt, scaffold, prepared lab, reading, or quiz is supporting
   evidence; it does not establish an accepted project.
10. When capacity is Red, perform one authorized 25-minute executable
    micro-step and capture its result or blocker rather than replacing practice
    with retrieval alone.

## Weekly Live Research Contract

This contract applies to every week containing cybersecurity study.

1. Start by attempting the week's smallest authorized incident project. Record
   the actual topic, acceptance check, observed blocker, and evidence needed.
   Then record its concepts and mechanisms,
   expected depth, practical skill, CEH / CEHP overlap, value beyond
   certification, and developments that may change the traditional account.
2. Run a topic-driven live web scan during the same week and record its date
   and timezone. Use it to answer the observed blocker, safety question, or
   acceptance question. If the first attempt exposes no blocker, verify one
   current authoritative mechanism relevant to the slice. Remembered sources
   and static reading lists do not satisfy the gate. Refresh the scan when the
   topic changes materially. If live access is unavailable, mark the scan
   incomplete and leave current/frontier claims open.
3. Prefer the evidence chain: original paper or standard, official vendor or
   government advisory, CVE / CISA / CERT record, technical analysis, then
   secondary reporting for discovery. Search the relevant mix of top or
   specialized conferences, journals, technical research teams, and real-world
   exploitation records; use no source-class quota and include AI / agent
   security only when the mechanism genuinely connects.
4. Curate for information value, normally `5–8` resources. For each resource
   record title, publication, date, category, evidence class, why it matters
   this week, `1–3` learning targets, and one priority: `MUST READ`,
   `SHOULD READ`, or `OPTIONAL / DEEP DIVE`.
5. Separate foundational knowledge from the current frontier. Label established
   fact, peer-reviewed evidence, statutory or standards text, vendor claim,
   threat-intelligence assessment, proof of concept, anecdote, and inference
   accurately; state uncertainty or disagreement when it affects the lesson.
6. Include a `Connection to This Week's Learning` map covering certification
   concept to real implementation, attack to modern variant, defense to known
   failure mode, theory to vulnerability, exploitation to detection and
   response, and traditional security to AI / agent security where meaningful.
   State what a CEH-only treatment would leave outdated or incomplete.
7. When reasonable, turn one source into a lawful, authorized, isolated
   exercise. Specify objective, environment, expected result, and evidence to
   save. Proposed work stays distinct from an experiment Jason completed.
8. Store the durable brief at `research-briefs/YYYY-Www.md`. Use the stable
   sections `A` through `L`: why the topic matters, core knowledge,
   certification connection, dated live scan, curated research, current
   real-world example, state of the art, meaningful AI / agent connection,
   hands-on exercise, research question, durable output, and definition of
   done. Within them preserve the weekly topic, learned concepts, important
   research and technical article, real-world case, attack and defense,
   experiment state, changed understanding, open questions, ideas, and
   references.
9. Ground research ideas in reviewed evidence. Record problem, existing
   approach, limitation, potential gap, hypothesis, experiment, contribution,
   difficulty, and resources; label each `Observation`, `Early Hypothesis`,
   `Research Candidate`, or `Strong Research Direction`. `No supported
   candidate` is valid. Route deeper ideation to `../brainstorming-lab` only
   after explicit activation.
10. Record the professional capability developed and the three horizons:
    immediate weekly understanding, professional security-engineering ability,
    and a plausible `2–5` year frontier question. At each curriculum-phase
    start, and at least every 12 weeks during a longer phase, check official
    current role listings and extract stable capability patterns without
    overfitting to one opening.
11. Stop searching once the topic has adequate foundational, current,
    real-world, defensive, and relevant frontier coverage. Unrelated news and
    vulnerabilities stay outside the weekly scope.
12. Keep public briefs privacy-safe: exclude credentials, personal identifiers,
    restricted evidence, real target details, and instructions that enable
    activity outside authorized isolated environments.

## Evidence Boundary

Active evidence:

- official course links;
- redacted registration facts;
- personal study notes;
- pretest / posttest answers;
- authorized lab observations;
- incident-project code, commands, logs, hashes, decisions, and independently
  reproduced project-linked CTF evidence.

Protected boundaries:

- national ID, birthday, phone, email, card data;
- passwords, API keys, private tokens;
- authorized training targets and local toy examples;
- lawful lab procedures aligned with official training environments.
